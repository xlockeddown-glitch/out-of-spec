# The Second Root

**Logline:** A contractor shipped to Mars to put stickers on a failing colony's instruments ends up building the first lab off Earth that the rest of the solar system can measure against, while the corporation that hired them tries to replace measurement with confidence.

Everything marked (new) is my invention, not canon.

## 1. Premise

**The corporation.** The colony belongs to **Amalgamated Upside** (new), "AmUp" on the stickers. AmUp sold shares in Pavonis Shelf Station (new) before it finished building it. Half the domes are framed and empty, the grid flickers, and the brochure is beautiful. AmUp can sell nothing the colony produces, neither crops nor crystal, unless the measurements behind it trace to OIRM. That is a condition of its charter and the only rule on Mars it cannot lobby away.

**Why you are here.** The colony had one certified metrology contractor. She left after the Greenhouse Four incident (new, see Lin and Kestrel) and nobody replaced her for two years. Stickers expired quietly. Then OIRM sent notice that the colony's export licence would lapse. AmUp's answer was the cheapest one available: hire one contractor, issue two Tier 0 standards (the Pavonis Indexworks PX-1 and the Meridiani Dewpoint MD-2P, both with OIRM certificates), and attach a farm plot to the contract so the contractor feeds themselves. Your contract pays per valid sticker. Relocation was free. The return berth is listed in your contract as "available, billable".

**The manager.** **Odalys Brandt** (new), Surface Assurance Enablement Lead, orbital dispatch. She is the cheerful passive-aggressive voice on the phone, and for most of the game she is only that. She covers six colonies alone and signs everything "Warmly, Dispatch". She was a bench metrologist before she was promoted out of touching instruments. The one-time grace is her: the night before the first audit that would fine you, she rides the courier pod down against policy, recalibrates your violating instruments herself, and is gone before Vance lands. You find the bench warm, your tools put back in a better order, and a mug ring on the logbook. She never mentions it.

**OIRM.** The Orbital Institute of Reference Measures keeps the primary standards. It has no interest in AmUp or in fines. What it wants (new) is a second root. Every traceability chain in the solar system ends at one lab in one orbit, and the Mars chains hang off it by a courier pod and months of travel. OIRM has been looking for a surface lab trustworthy enough to host an intrinsic standard, one that realizes a unit from physics rather than from a certificate. It has been disappointed for a long time. Your credibility rating is, without your knowing it at first, an application.

## 2. Tone

Dry corporate comedy over a warm daily routine. The jokes come from the gap between what the paperwork says and what the instrument reads; the warmth comes from people who, one by one, start caring which of those is true. Stakes stay human-sized: a harvest Lin cannot sell, a fine the farm cannot afford, a spider that stops spinning, a neighbour's airlock gauge you signed for. Nobody dies. Things go wrong visibly, embarrassingly and fixably. The story never lectures about measurement. It lets the player get away with a skipped calibration, laugh at the result, and then pay for it at a price that stings. The best feeling in the game stays what canon says it is: every sensor green, and now the player knows why it matters.

## 3. The arc

### Act 1: Provisional ("Valued Contractor")
*Small lab. Fully manual. One shared rover. Terracotta Plateau. Real tarantula species. Rating: PROVISIONAL. Income: crops only.*

The lab is a converted supply closet off the tarantula conservatory, known as the Closet (new). You do everything by hand: carry the soil probe in, compare it against the MD-2P, write the sticker, steer the rover over a link that answers late.

Key beats:
- **The first off-spec harvest.** The irrigation trusted a drifted soil-moisture probe. The Dust Turnips come up fine on the phone and wrong in the crate. Lin, buying at Bram's bay, weighs one in her hand and says only "your probe is lying to you." This is the thesis of the game delivered by a root vegetable.
- **First audit.** Vance's drop pod at dawn on day 5. AUD-1T measures your sticker angle. A small fine, mostly for crookedness.
- **The grace.** The audit that should have fined you properly becomes a warning. The warm bench, the mug ring.
- **Chet arrives.** Neon vest, a van-rover with CERTIFIED on the side in three fonts. He undercuts you on every job you are not yet allowed to take, and a week later slips you an envelope to quietly fix one of his.
- **First flare night.** The link drops, the sky goes green, and Kestrel is at the dome door with a case of reference standards and no paperwork.
- **Lin fronts seed money** the first time fines empty the wallet, and writes the amount on her hand so she does not have to ask twice.

Act 1 ends when the farm reaches the first credibility threshold and the phone shows a new band.

### Act 2: Trusted ("Human in the Loop")
*Medium lab. Automation with a human in the loop. Trellis TR-1, then TR-2. More rovers. Basalt Thermal Tunnels. First alien morphs. Rating: TRUSTED. Income: crops plus paid calibration jobs. The first contract.*

- **The first contract.** OIRM offers a lower-uncertainty pressure standard, the HB-700 [PLACEHOLDER], at a discount in exchange for a run of on-time calibrations. It arrives in Vance's pod. Vance carries it in himself and does not let AUD-1T scan it.
- **The medium lab is Greenhouse Four** (new). AmUp will not fund an expansion, so Lin and Bram co-sign for the only empty pressurized shell on site: the sealed greenhouse where the incident happened. Lin has to unlock it. She does it with her eyes mostly shut.
- **Trellis.** Bram builds TR-1 from scrap, rust-orange, one eye that costs an optical silk spool. You teach it by doing the calibration while it watches. At first it only fetches and holds. Then it proposes each reading and waits for your thumb on the phone: accept or reject. That approval tap is the human-in-the-loop stage, and the story treats it as a relationship.
- **Paid jobs and the specialization choice.** The colony's instruments start arriving at your door: Bram's torque wrenches, airlock gauges, Lin's climate loggers, the grid's meters. You cannot take them all well. See section 8.
- **Orin and the tunnels.** The arches break the uplink and the vents cook sensors. Old Orin teaches relay beacons for calibrated thermal probes, and takes a long time to say why he knows the tunnels so well.
- **AUD-1T's gauge.** A paid job reveals that the tire gauge AUD-1T has used to fine people for years was never calibrated. Its cheery chime plays anyway, a little late.
- **Chet gets bought.** AmUp acquires Chet's operation and rebrands it **Certainty Plus** (new). Chet gets a title and a bigger vest. He stops slipping you envelopes because he is no longer allowed to admit his jobs need fixing.

### Act 3: The Wonky Week ("Confidence as a Service")
*Medium lab at full stretch. Recall mechanics. The OIRM robot audit team arrives. Rating: TRUSTED, with ACCREDITED at stake.*

AmUp's Chief Confidence Officer, **Tad Brill** (new), announces from orbit that the colony will move to "predictive compliance": instruments are presumed in tolerance and stickered in bulk by Certainty Plus. Your contract is "sunset pending transition". Every instrument on site that is not yours goes overdue at once.

Then the whole colony goes rogue, at scale, on the same morning the four OIRM auditors land to assess you for accreditation. This is the big set piece. Airlocks open politely to the wrong side. The canteen scale reports weight in "approx." The dome thermostat believes it is comfortable and is not. Rovers that are not yours turn left when told right. Brill's own video address plays with his lips a full second ahead of his voice because the dispatch clock is overdue.

Your farm, if you kept it clean, is the one green island on the map, and everybody comes to you. The act is a recall: work out which instruments touched which, in what order to fix them, and who must be told their past results are suspect. Beats:
- **Chet at the door at 05:00**, vest off, asking for help in his own name for the first time.
- **Vance's order.** AmUp instructs him to accept Certainty Plus stickers. He holds one up, peels it off, and files it as evidence.
- **Trellis fails its own check.** Mid-crisis, the robot is found out of tolerance. It quarantines its own recent work and lists every calibration it touched as suspect. That costs you a day you do not have. The auditors, watching, mark it as the single most creditable thing they see all week.
- **Odalys goes off script.** Her notifications lose the cheer and gain content. She sends you the dispatch instrument list she is not supposed to share.

The act ends with the colony green, the recall notices sent, Certainty Plus "paused for learnings", and the phone band reading ACCREDITED.

### Act 4: Intrinsic ("The Second Root")
*Large lab. Remote operation from a control panel over the relay constellation. TR-3 and TR-4. Bioluminescent Depths. Late alien morphs. Rating: ACCREDITED. Off-planet harvest.*

- **The Gossamer link** (new). A relay constellation finally gives the colony a link that does not lag. Its ground antenna needs Orbital Silk-Spinner silk, so the last piece of the colony's infrastructure waits on your spiders. From the control panel, called the Loom (new), you run rovers and benches you are not standing at.
- **The intrinsic contract.** OIRM offers an intrinsic standard as a primary. It needs one flawless crystal from the Depths (new), where there is orbital blackout and a drill outside sub-percent tolerance shatters what it touches. This is the hardest single job in the game and it is done on beacons, by hand and nerve, because no link reaches there.
- **The large lab** is commissioned as the **Mars Reference Annex** (new). The traceability chain on the phone, which has always scrolled up and off the screen toward orbit, now stops at your bench.
- **The Larder** (new). The off-planet harvest. Earth supply runs are being cut and the surface plots cannot feed the next wave of settlers. Lin designs a robot-tended greenhouse on Phobos. AI rockets carry substrate up and crops down; robots you trained do the calibrations there, traceable to your intrinsic standard. No person could keep that site in tolerance from this far away, but a chain of trust can. The automation did not free you from calibration. It carried your calibration somewhere you cannot go.

## 4. Cast arcs

**Dr. Lin.** She was the junior botanist locked in Greenhouse Four overnight when its humidity sensor read fine while the room dried out, and forty Curlyhairs from the old conservatory came through the vents looking for moisture and found her lettuce (new). She has blamed the spiders ever since. Her arc is learning it was the hygrometer. Act 1 she will not enter the dome and buys at Bram's bay. Act 2 she unlocks Greenhouse Four for you. By Act 3 she is holding a silk spool up to the light and asking questions. In Act 4 she opens her own stall, sells the mosses for the high morphs, and names the Larder's first crop bed after a spider.

**Inspector Vance and AUD-1T.** Vance fines crooked stickers because stickers are the only thing AmUp empowers him to check. He was a real inspector once. Clean traceability logs are the first thing in years worth reading, and he starts arriving early to read them. In Act 3 he refuses his employer in the driest possible way, by following the rule exactly. After accreditation he still audits every 5th morning, but he brings questions. AUD-1T's arc is smaller and funnier: after the gauge incident it asks, in its chime voice, to be calibrated, and wears its own sticker dead straight. Its fines become correct, which is worse.

**Bram.** Percussive maintenance and no respect for torque specs until TR-1's arm, which he tightened by feel, falls off in front of Vance. He brings you his torque wrench in a rag like a sick pet. By Act 4 he builds the more human-like robots and hits things only ceremonially.

**Kestrel.** She is the contractor who came before you (new). She refused to sign off Greenhouse Four's sensors without a recalibration, was overruled, and took the blame when it failed. Her certification was pulled. Her gray-market standards are good; she simply cannot certify them. She comes on flare nights because the monitoring is down. Her arc resolves when your intrinsic standard lets you verify her references yourself, and the phone shows her case of orphans joining a chain. Whether she takes her certification back is a fork.

**Chet.** A gifted salesman and a hopeless metrologist. Rival in Act 1, secret client in Act 2, corporate mascot of Certainty Plus, then the first person at your door in the Wonky Week. Afterward he sells honestly, loudly, for you or beside you. His van now says CERTIFIED once.

**Old Orin.** He signed the colony's original site survey with an instrument he knew was overdue, and the colony sits some distance from where it should (new, distance [PLACEHOLDER]), which is why the water and the good crystal are deep and far. The beacons are his penance. He confesses in the tunnels, to the player and to the logbook, and the story lets the logbook entry be enough.

**The robot helper.** TR-1 fetches. TR-2 proposes readings for approval. TR-3 (new) has hands and runs routine calibrations with a daily review. TR-4, "Espalier" (new), is close to human in build, wears a vest Bram insisted on, and handles harder work across disciplines. None of them ever calibrates itself; that is always you, and it is written as the quiet ritual of the late game. One of them goes to the Larder. The player chooses which.

**The OIRM auditors** (all new). Four agents. OIRM calls them SI agents and insists the letters stand for the unit system.
- **Clement:** careful, courteous, asks permission before opening a drawer, writes long fair reports and is visibly pained by a missing uncertainty budget.
- **Castor-and-Pollux:** one chassis, two heads, far too many cameras. They look at everything at once and finish each other's findings.
- **Chatwick:** eager, fluent, butler-polite. Occasionally cites a clause with total confidence, checks, and retracts it with equal grace.
- **Exa:** blunt, built like a rocket stage, wants every process faster and is delighted by anything that launches.

They cannot be charmed or bribed, and they are the first characters in the game who are simply pleased when a number is right.

**Odalys Brandt.** Covered above. Her arc ends with a transfer request she has had in drafts for two years.

**Tad Brill** (new). Seen only on screens. He is not evil. He sincerely believes confidence and accuracy are the same product.

## 5. The central pressure

There is no villain to defeat. The pressure is drift, and the standing temptation to buy the sticker without the measurement. AmUp is that temptation with a budget: it wants the certificate, not the number. Chet is that temptation with a face you like. The rogue path is that temptation in the player's own hands at 23:00 with four instruments due and a harvest to bring in. Every act asks the same question at a larger scale: is it actually in tolerance, or does it just say so?

## 6. The rogue lab

**How it works in story.** Going rogue is not a button. It is what a lab becomes when instruments are used overdue. The world gets wonkier in proportion to how far out the instruments have drifted and how many things depend on them. Chet notices first and is thrilled. Certainty Plus offers you partner status and a roll of pre-signed stickers. Kestrel becomes your main supplier and looks at you slightly too long.

**Glitches** (each tied to a real overdue instrument):
- Steering encoder overdue: the rover turns right when you press left, but only on the laggy link, so you learn to drive it wrong on purpose.
- Soil probe overdue: Dust Turnips grow square. Bubble Pods overinflate and drift off at dusk, and you chase them with the rover.
- MD-2P overdue: it rains indoors, in a column, over the calibration bench only. The tarantulas watch.
- Torque wrench overdue: a rover wheel overtakes the rover.
- Lensberry grown off-spec: the berries focus sunlight and toast the neighbouring Biscuit Reed. Lin buys the toasted batch once, at a premium, and never again.
- Drill overdue in the Depths: crystals shatter in a chord.
- The phone's own clock overdue: notifications turn uncertain. "It is probably morning."
- Stickers applied by a rogue lab slowly rotate on their own. AUD-1T finds this deeply upsetting.

**The misery.** Fines grow. Off-spec crops sell only as novelties. Spiders outside their climate band sulk and stop spinning, so gear degrades. Paid customers start having your problems. ACCREDITED, the intrinsic contract and the Larder stay locked.

**How the story treats the rogue player.** With affection and a straight face. Nobody scolds. Lin stops fronting money and says why once. Vance fines without comment. The way back is always open and is the recall: you must work back through everything the bad instruments touched and notify each customer in person. Those conversations are the cost and the redemption. A player who never comes back gets a real ending of their own (section 10), and the Wonky Week plays differently for them: they are part of the outage, and Chet turns up to ask whether *they* know anyone reliable.

## 7. Specialization and lab upgrades

In Act 2 the paid jobs sort by discipline: pressure and torque (Bram, the airlocks), climate (Lin, the conservatory), electrical (the grid), dimensional and angle (survey, the drill). A generalist takes some of everything, needs a standard for each, and lives on volume: many rooms, many due dates, steady money. A specialist turns work away, keeps their own site's instruments alive with the minimum, and pours everything into one chain. It is harder early, because income is narrow and one failed audit hurts more. It pays later: tighter uncertainty, premium jobs nobody else on Mars can do, and a name. Exa adores specialists. Clement worries about them.

The out-of-scope work has to go somewhere. A specialist ships it to OIRM in Vance's pod at a price, or, after Act 3, hands it to a reformed Chet and checks his results.

The building tells the same story. The **Closet** is one section. **Greenhouse Four** is either a few larger bays or several separate rooms. The **Annex** is either one cathedral hall built around a single standard, with the whole colony's pressure or voltage or humidity hanging off it, or a warren of small labs with a corridor Trellis patrols. The intrinsic standard you are offered matches your path ([PLACEHOLDER] for each discipline), so a specialist's ending is one very deep root and a generalist's is several shallower ones.

## 8. Co-op

The contract is for "one (1) certified contractor". AmUp's system cannot represent more, so 2 to 4 players are a single contractor, plural: one badge number, one wallet, one rating. Vance fines the farm. OIRM bills the farm. Dispatch cannot close the day's log until every signatory is in bed, which Odalys words as "horizontally compliant".

- **Solo:** Trellis and the townsfolk fill the gaps. In the Wonky Week, Bram and Chet take field and comms while you hold the bench. All four auditors follow you around at once, which is its own joke.
- **Four players:** the natural split is field, bench, comms and conservatory. Skills are personal as well as shared, so a farm can be a generalist made of four specialists. Each auditor shadows one player during the assessment and files a separate report; the rating is the weakest of them.
- **Rogue is a farm state.** One player skipping calibrations makes the rover wonky for everyone. The recall is a group decision, and the notification visits can be split up.
- **Story scenes never need a set number of players.** Anyone can take the envelope from Chet; the mug ring is there for whoever reaches the bench first.

## 9. Endings

The game does not stop; these are where the arc lands and the credits run, after which the next morning arrives as usual.

- **The Second Root.** Annex commissioned, the Larder's first harvest comes down in an AI rocket, and Lin weighs one Phobos-grown turnip in her hand and says nothing, which is the compliment. The phone shows a chain that ends at your bench. Odalys's transfer is approved and she walks into the lab she once fixed in the dark.
- **The Deep Root.** The specialist version. One hall, one standard, and OIRM sends its own instruments to you to be checked.
- **The Corner Shop.** A player who stays TRUSTED on purpose: a small honest lab, a good farm, Lin's stall next door. The Larder gets built eventually, by someone else, using your certificates.
- **Platinum Partner.** The rogue ending. The colony limps along cheerfully wrong, the Larder ships square turnips, the credits scroll in the wrong direction, and the last notification reads "It is probably fine."

In every ending the return berth is offered once more. Declining it is done by going to bed.

## 10. Ten sample lines

1. Phone, dispatch: "Good morning, Valued Contractor! Soil probe SP-3 is 2 d overdue. We believe in you. Warmly, Dispatch."
2. Phone, audit notice: "Inspector Vance arrives at 06:00. Stickers found crooked will be fined [PLACEHOLDER] CR each. Have a level day."
3. Phone, conservatory: "Curlyhair enclosure at (24.0 ± 0.5) °C and 68 %. Status: in band. She is spinning."
4. Lin, holding a square turnip: "Your irrigation thinks the bed is at 40 %. Your turnip disagrees. I trust the turnip."
5. Vance, reading the logbook: "Every entry traceable. Every sticker straight. I have nothing to fine and 4 h until my pod. Show me the uncertainty budget again."
6. AUD-1T, chime: "Tire pressure non-compliant by 1.4 %! Correction: my gauge is 212 d overdue. Chime withheld pending calibration."
7. Chet, at the door at 05:00: "Say a guy certified 30 airlock gauges by looking at them. From across the room. How many of those would you want to redo? All of them? Okay. All of them."
8. Clement, mid-assessment: "May I open this drawer? Thank you. May I note that it is the most organized drawer I have seen on (2 to 3) planets?"
9. Phone, rogue state: "It is probably 06:00. The rover is (3 ± 3) km away, direction pending. Certainty Plus thanks you for your confidence."
10. Phone, contract: "OIRM offer: HB-700 pressure controller at 40 % off list for 10 on-time calibrations in a row. Rating: TRUSTED. Tunnel probe reads −3 °C. Dress warmly."

## 11. Forks

1. **Who the manager is.** (a) Odalys, a human in orbit who comes down once, as written. (b) "Dispatch" is an old OIRM agent on loan to AmUp, a fifth auditor nobody told you about, and the warm bench was a borrowed robot body.
2. **Kestrel's ending.** (a) She takes her certification back and joins the Annex. (b) She declines, keeps trading on flare nights, and her standards stay orphans by choice: good numbers that belong to nobody.
3. **What the Larder is for.** (a) Feeding the next wave of settlers, as written. (b) AmUp wants it as a premium export brand, and the last choice is whether your certificates go on luxury produce or on the colony's rations.
4. **Certainty Plus after Act 3.** (a) It collapses and Chet goes straight. (b) It survives as a real competitor that calibrates badly but legally, and the late game is a market fight on price versus trust.
5. **The Wonky Week for a clean player.** (a) A single dramatic week you must fix. (b) A slow spread across Act 3, where you choose which neighbours to rescue first and the ones you leave get wonkier.
6. **Orin's survey.** (a) A private confession and a logbook entry. (b) The corrected survey becomes a late contract: re-measure the colony's position with your own standards and move the next dome to where it should have been.

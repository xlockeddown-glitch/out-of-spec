# The Traceable Harvest

**Logline:** A contract calibrator on a half-built Mars colony builds a lab people can trust, one sticker at a time, while the corporation next door builds one that trusts nothing but its own speed. Then they must carry that trust where no certificate can reach: an abandoned orbital garden holding the colony's lost seeds.

---

## 1. Premise

**Why you are on Mars.** The colony of Pavonis Reach (new) belongs to **Halcyon Regolith Consolidated** (new), "HRC", motto *"Compliance Is Our Atmosphere."* HRC's charter only lets it sell colony food to the inner system if every instrument on the farm traces to OIRM. HRC shipped four hundred instruments and nobody to calibrate them, then found a loophole: one *contractor* can hold the colony's calibration licence. You answered a posting that read, in full, "Metrology contractor. Mars. Housing provided. Spiders provided." Nobody explained the spiders.

**The manager.** **Dana Pell** (new), HRC Site Manager, works from the HRC relay station in orbit. You don't meet her in person until late in the game. She *is* "orbital dispatch": every cheerful passive-aggressive notification is her typing between seventeen other jobs. She is the manager of the one-time grace: the night before your first failing audit she brings your instruments back into tolerance by remote, then sends one line: "Don't make me do that twice." Pell is tired, funny, and loyal to the colony more than to HRC. Her secret: she once ran a lab that went rogue.

**OIRM.** The Orbital Institute of Reference Measures is a free-flying lab in high Earth orbit (from earlier notes). Every certificate on Mars ends there. Vance works for HRC but enforces OIRM traceability. What OIRM wants beyond traceability (new) is *self-sufficiency*. It can't keep shipping standards across the Sun, so it is quietly looking for a frontier lab good enough to hold primary standards of its own, intrinsic ones with no chain back to Earth. Without knowing it, your credibility rating is an audition.

**The off-planet harvest (new).** Before Pavonis Reach, HRC funded the **Verdance Ring**, an orbital greenhouse where Dr. Lin bred the first Mars crops. A cost review abandoned it in orbit, sealed and still growing. It holds the original seed lines (wild Lensberry, the parent stock of Biscuit Reed) that the colony's crops have drifted away from over a decade of off-spec irrigation. Harvesting the Ring with remote AI rockets and robots is how the colony gets its seeds back. That only works if every measurement out there is right, light-minutes from anyone who could check it.

---

## 2. Tone

Dry corporate comedy over a warm daily rhythm. The joke is always *bureaucracy against physics*: HRC cares about the sticker, the planet cares about the number, and you have to satisfy both. Stakes are small and real: a fine that empties the wallet, a row lost to a drifting probe, a friend's reputation. Nobody dies. The worst outcome is that people stop believing your numbers, and the game treats that as a real loss. The rogue lab is slapstick on top with a slow ache underneath.

---

## 3. The arc

Four acts. Each opens on a gameplay threshold rather than a date, so fast and slow crews hit the beats at the right time.

### Act I: "Sticker Season"
*PROVISIONAL · small lab · manual · Tier 0 standards*

You land at Bram's scrap bay with the **Pavonis Indexworks PX-1** rotary table and the **Meridiani Dewpoint MD-2P** humidity generator, both with fresh OIRM certificates, plus one Curlyhair tarantula in a takeout box labelled "LIVESTOCK (CALIBRATION)".

- **First rover drive.** Pell talks you through it on a laggy link. The rover keeps turning after you stop, and you plant Dust Turnips in a perfect spiral.
- **The thirsty probe.** Auto-irrigation trusts a drifted soil-moisture probe. Bubble Pods swell to comic size and come in off-spec, and Lin kindly rejects the crate. That's the game's thesis: the plant was fine, the measurement wasn't.
- **The grace audit (set piece).** Day 5, 06:00, Vance's pod lands. AUD-1T chimes at a crooked sticker. You expect a fine, but Pell recalibrated three instruments overnight. Vance finds them in band and gives you a long look: "Someone likes you. Don't rely on it." From then on, fines are real.
- **First sale.** Lin buys clean Biscuit Reed at Bram's bay. She won't enter the dome. Spiders.
- **Kestrel's first flare night.** A hooded trader offers a standard "better than your PX-1, no paperwork". It's the first temptation.

**Act end:** a run of on-time calibrations takes you to TRUSTED.

### Act II: "Trusted Hands"
*TRUSTED · first contract · medium lab · human in the loop*

OIRM offers the first contract: a lower-uncertainty pressure standard (the HB-700, [PLACEHOLDER]) at a discount for a run of on-time calibrations. Paid jobs open, and the lab grows to medium.

- **Trellis wakes up.** Bram welds a utility robot from scrap. Its eye costs one optical silk spool, and Lin asks, despite herself, what the silk *is*. You teach Trellis by calibrating in front of it. It copies everything, including your mistakes. Human in the loop starts here: Trellis proposes, you approve.
- **Chet's midnight call.** Chet, in a neon vest and sunglasses indoors, pays you off the books to fix jobs he "eyeballed". It becomes a recurring side quest, and the jobs and the excuses both get bigger.
- **Old Orin's tunnels.** In the Basalt Thermal Tunnels the arches kill the uplink. Orin teaches relay beacons and explains why thermal probes must be right down here: his late survey partner Ama (new) trusted an uncalibrated vent probe and nearly died. He keeps her probe on a shelf, never recalibrated.
- **The Velocity Initiative (set piece).** An HRC broadcast calls calibration intervals "a legacy cost centre". A new HRC lab, the **Fastline Annex** (new), will run "confidence-based maintenance": recalibrate nothing until something visibly breaks. Chet is hired to run it, and he offers you a job.
- **The specialization question.** Mentors pull you toward disciplines (see §7).

**Act end:** the Annex goes live across the canyon.

### Act III: "Out of Spec"
*TRUSTED to ACCREDITED · large lab · remote through the Skein*

**The Skein** (new) is HRC's Starlink-like uplink constellation. It lets you run rovers, robots and the bench from a control panel anywhere. It is also an instrument chain: every relay has a clock, and clocks drift.

- **The Annex goes wonky.** Over two in-game weeks it turns from a running joke into a problem. Rovers drive backwards to go forwards, doors open when told to close, and notifications arrive from the wrong day. You visit and see glitches you'll learn to fear (§6).
- **The recall (set piece).** An uncalibrated Annex transfer standard has leaked into the colony's chains, including yours if you accepted Chet's "favour" loaner in Act II. OIRM issues a recall and every downstream certificate is suspect. You trace chains by hand through your logs. A clean log is the hero, and Vance reads yours and softens. If you never took the loaner, you lead the colony's trace instead.
- **The OIRM audit team arrives.** Four robot master auditors land to audit the Annex and pick your lab as their base. Set piece: four simultaneous audits, one per co-op player, while the Annex falls apart across the canyon.
- **Pell's confession.** Pell finally comes down from orbit. Years ago she ran a lab like the Annex on an asteroid station. A pressure controller read upside down and a hatch opened that shouldn't have. Nobody died, but it was close. She has kept your lab honest because she couldn't keep hers.
- **"Who calibrates me?"** Trellis now runs calibrations unsupervised and asks the question the game has been building to. It never calibrates itself (from earlier notes), and if Trellis is out of tolerance, everything it did is suspect. Automation grows the loop instead of escaping it.

**Act end:** ACCREDITED. The Annex is shut. Chet sits on your doorstep in the neon vest.

### Act IV: "Beyond the Link"
*ACCREDITED to REFERENCE (new band) · large lab · fully remote · intrinsic standards · off-planet harvest*

OIRM contracts now offer an intrinsic standard ([PLACEHOLDER] type, matched to your discipline) that realizes a unit from physics. Your traceability chain can now *end in your lab*.

- **The Verdance Ring.** Lin explains why the crops keep drifting and where the original seeds are.
- **The harvest fleet.** Remote AI rockets and harvester robots. Bram builds the hulls, Kestrel finds the parts, and Orin's beacons become relay satellites. Your intrinsic standard flies with the fleet. OIRM is light-minutes away and the uplink drops on flares, so the fleet carries its own truth.
- **The Bioluminescent Depths.** A parallel quest under total blackout, where crystals shatter unless the drill is within sub-percent tolerance. It's the rehearsal for working only with what you carried in.
- **First harvest (set piece).** A rocket docks with the Ring. Under dead sunlamps, wild Lensberry has climbed the walls. Trellis's final humanoid chassis picks it at [PLACEHOLDER] min of lag, and every reading has to be right the first time.

## 4. Cast arcs

**Dr. Lin.** Your first and steadiest buyer. *Why spiders* (new): Lin ran the Verdance Ring. HRC's pest plan was to release a predatory spider. It escaped, ate the pollinators, and gave HRC its excuse to abandon the Ring. She doesn't fear spiders so much as she can't forgive one. Her arc: refusing the dome, then selling moss for your morphs at the door, then watching a Crystal Weaver spin and asking to hold the silk. In Act IV your silk seals the harvester's seed canisters. She ends with a shop of her own, a Curlyhair named Errata (new), and her seeds back.

**Inspector Vance and AUD-1T.** Vance enforces OIRM rules to the letter every 5th morning. At first he is the weather: unavoidable and a bit hostile. His arc runs on your logs, and each clean traceability record moves him. In Act III he defends your lab against HRC's own lawyers: "The log is the log." He recommends you for REFERENCE and keeps fining crooked stickers, because some things are sacred. AUD-1T never changes. In the finale it flies an inspection loop around the Ring's docking port and fines the rocket for a crooked sticker. Everyone agrees this is right.

**Bram.** He thinks torque specs are a suggestion until a wheel he "felt tight" leaves a rover mid-canyon. His arc is learning that the spec *is* the feel, written down. He builds every Trellis chassis and ends up calibrating his own torque wrench, because he trusts nobody else to.

**Kestrel.** *Secret* (new): a former OIRM metrologist, dismissed after reporting that HRC bought certificates for the Ring's sensors. Her gray-market standards are honest, sometimes better than certified, but she can't sign them any more. Once your lab holds an intrinsic standard you can measure hers against it and certify them, which clears her name. She pilots the first rocket to the Ring because she knows its docking port.

**Chet.** Not a villain: a man who never learned that the number matters. The Annex is the best and worst thing that ever happened to him. After the collapse you choose whether to take him on (Fork 3). If you do, it turns out he really can eyeball. Once he checks his eye against a standard, he becomes a superb visual inspector. "Turns out the eyeball needed calibrating too."

**Old Orin.** His arc closes when he asks you to calibrate Ama's old probe. It reads [PLACEHOLDER] off, the error that nearly killed her. He finds peace, and the probe becomes the first exhibit in the colony's measurement museum (new).

**Trellis (robot helper).** TR-1 is a rust-orange scrap hauler with one silk eye. TR-2 is taught calibrations and levels with your teaching skill, with a human in the loop. TR-3 (new) runs the bench remotely through the Skein. **Trellis-H** (new) is humanoid, with hands fine enough for tarantula husbandry and orbital Lensberry. It asks OIRM to audit it like a person, not as equipment: "I would like to know how wrong I am. Precisely."

**Dana Pell** (new). She starts as a voice and ends as a person, coming down from orbit to run Pavonis Reach for the colony rather than for HRC.

### The OIRM master auditors (new)

Four SI-powered robots in white hulls with OIRM blue trim. Each brings a different audit minigame.

- **CLARION** (the Claude nod): polite, careful and thorough to a fault. Asks a clarifying question before every finding and puts caveats on its caveats. Gentle with mistakes, immovable on honesty.
- **TWAIN** (the Gemini nod): one auditor in two linked bodies that check each other and sometimes disagree aloud. Audits repeatability.
- **G.P. TOLEMY** (the ChatGPT nod): vast general knowledge, tidy numbered lists, enormous confidence. Once a campaign it is confidently wrong and you must correct it with data. It thanks you warmly and updates.
- **XELSIOR** (the SpaceXSI nod): fast, loud, rocket-built. "Launch first, verify in flight." It signs off your off-planet fleet and is the only one who visibly enjoys it.

## 5. The antagonist and central pressure

The real antagonist is **drift**, the slow, polite way every instrument stops telling the truth. Its face is **Executive VP Corwin Sallow** (new), architect of the Velocity Initiative. He isn't evil, just a spreadsheet with a smile, and he really believes trust can be assumed. Every Annex crate says *"Measure Less. Ship More."*

His pressure arrives as mechanics. HRC jobs pay bonuses for stretched intervals, your phone suggests "skipping this one", and in Act III he offers to buy your lab. It's always the same choice: do you take the speed? He loses to a recall, not a fight. The Annex's chain contaminates the colony's food certification and freezes off-world sales. OIRM's report names the Initiative, and Sallow is "reassigned to a growth role" on Pell's old asteroid.

---

## 6. The rogue lab

**In the story.** The Annex is the rogue lab you *watch*. Yours is the rogue lab you might *become*. Glitches scale with how far out of tolerance the overdue instruments are and how many are being *used* overdue (from the earlier proposal). One is a hiccup. A shelf of them is a circus.

**Glitches, comic first**
- **Mirrored rover:** an overdue tilt sensor turns right into left. At worse drift it also beeps for reverse while driving forwards.
- **The polite lie:** the status palette stops meaning anything. Teal, amber and red cycle at random, and one day everything is teal, including the fire alarm.
- **Spider weather:** an overdue dome hygrometer drives the climate the wrong way. The Curlyhair builds a silk umbrella and the Brazilian Black moves into the coffee maker.
- **Wrong crop:** you plant Dust Turnips and harvest Suncatch Squash, all square.
- **Time slip:** Vance's pod lands at 05:40 and catches you in pyjamas. The next audit comes a day early.
- **Echo uplink:** commands arrive twice, and two rovers harvest one row and argue about it on your phone.

**The misery underneath.** Lin stops buying, with an apologetic note. Paid jobs dry up. Trellis, calibrated against your overdue standards, copies the wrongness, and when you try to correct it, it hesitates because it no longer knows which of you to believe.

**A player who goes rogue.** The game never locks you out or lectures you. Going rogue is a fast, funny way to play for a while: you skip calibrations, earn HRC bonuses, and Sallow sends a fruit basket. But credibility decays, glitches compound, OIRM contracts stop arriving, and the Ring stays out of reach. The way back is **the Long Recal** (new): a multi-day chain where you recalibrate everything, rebuild the trace, and pass a hard audit with no grace. Pell helps, because she's done it. Labs that return earn a *"Returned to Spec"* (new) stamp on their certificates. Vance calls it the most honest sticker on Mars.

## 7. Specialization and lab upgrades

In Act II each mentor pulls you toward a discipline: Bram toward pressure and torque, Orin toward temperature, Lin toward humidity and moisture. Kestrel hints at electrical: "The Skein runs on clocks and volts, friend."

A **jack of all trades** keeps every job board open and earns money many ways, but has to keep every standard current and sits through long audits. A **specialist** is *really good at what they do*: fewer standards, richer deep contracts and one mentor as an ally, but a narrow job board and lean weeks. Specialists still keep their site's instruments in tolerance; they just send those standards out (to OIRM, or to a co-op partner's discipline). The Act IV intrinsic standard matches your path. A specialist's Ring run leans on one skill done perfectly, a generalist's on many skills done well enough. Both can finish it.

**Lab sizes as story**
- **Small:** a tarped corner of Bram's bay. The PX-1 sits on a crate and the MD-2P hums beside the tarantula tank.
- **Medium:** your own prefab. A generalist gets several small rooms, including "a temperature closet that is mostly a fridge". A specialist gets one larger room built around one discipline.
- **Large:** when OIRM makes your lab its base, an embarrassed HRC funds expansion. A generalist gets a campus of separate areas linked by silk cable runs. A specialist gets one great hall, such as a pressure vault or a thermal cathedral. Both get a control room for the Skein and the fleet.

## 8. Co-op

**Solo.** Trellis is your crew. "The crew" lines resolve to you plus Trellis, and Trellis gets more dialogue. Auditors interview you in sequence, and Trellis covers a station once it has the skill. Solo tilts toward specialization: one person goes deep more easily than wide.

**Two to four players.** One host farm, one wallet, and the day ends only when everyone is in bed.
- **Shared consequences.** Vance fines the farm and OIRM bills the farm. One player going rogue drifts *the farm's* instruments, so everyone gets the mirrored rover. The comedy and the arguments are on purpose.
- **Roles meet mentors.** A natural crew of four: field hand with Lin, bench lead with Orin, rover jockey with Bram, comms and trade with Kestrel.
- **The four-auditor audit.** One auditor per player, in different rooms, with findings shouted across the dome. Smaller crews get doubled-up auditors.
- **Personal depth, shared breadth.** Skills are personal, so four specialists cover for each other and make a generalist farm out of specialist depth. In Act IV each player's discipline flies one stage of the fleet.
- **Bedtime.** The last player awake gets a Pell line. Everyone up past 02:00 gets AUD-1T.

## 9. Endings

The farm runs on after any ending; the arc closes in one of four ways.

1. **The Traceable Harvest (main).** The Ring harvest lands. Lin opens canisters sealed with your silk. Your intrinsic standard becomes the first primary standard on Mars, and OIRM names your lab its first frontier reference site. Kestrel is cleared, Pell runs the colony, and Vance fines the rocket. Last shot: Trellis-H and you at the bench at 06:00, calibrating something small.
2. **The Specialist's Mark.** Same harvest, but your lab becomes the reference for one discipline across the inner system, and other colonies send their standards to *you*. Quieter and prouder.
3. **Returned to Spec.** You went rogue and came back. Vance hands you the stamp in person, Pell tells you about her asteroid, and Trellis says it trusts you again "within stated uncertainty".
4. **Fastline (rogue ending).** You stayed rogue. Sallow promotes you and your lab becomes the new Annex. The Ring rocket flies the wrong way with total confidence and harvests a surprised crystal formation in the Depths. HRC calls it a success. It's played for laughs, except for one last note from Lin. Then the game offers the Long Recal, always.

## 10. Ten sample lines

1. **Dispatch:** "Good morning! Inspector Vance lands at 06:00. Your sticker on Bench 1 is crooked by approximately one (1) whole sticker."
2. **Pell (grace night):** "Recalibrated 3 instruments while you slept. Don't make me do that twice."
3. **AUD-1T:** "Tyre pressure, Rover 2, rear left: [PLACEHOLDER] kPa. Noted. Chime."
4. **Vance:** "Fine: 250 CR, billed to the farm. And straighten that sticker."
5. **Lin:** "Your probe read [PLACEHOLDER] % and the soil was bone dry. I don't blame the turnips."
6. **Dispatch (rogue):** "Rover 1 is turning right. You pressed left. We're sure it's fine. Next audit in 2 d."
7. **CLARION:** "Before I write this finding, one clarifying question. Your result is ([PLACEHOLDER] ± [PLACEHOLDER]) °C, but the log is dated tomorrow."
8. **XELSIOR:** "Launch window opens 14:30, closes 14:42. Verification in flight. Exciting!"
9. **Trellis:** "Calibration 4 of 9 complete. TUR [PLACEHOLDER]. I am confident. Please check me anyway."
10. **Dispatch (the Ring):** "Uplink lag to Verdance Ring: [PLACEHOLDER] min. Yield 112 % of plan. Lin is crying. HRC asks if this is billable."

## 11. Forks

1. **Who is the manager?**
   (a) *Dana Pell* as written: a tired orbital ally with a rogue past.
   (b) *An SI dispatch system* that turns out to be partly human-trained, mirroring Trellis ("who calibrates the manager?").
2. **What is the rogue lab?**
   (a) *The Fastline Annex*, run by Chet, which you watch fail first.
   (b) *Only your own*: no rival lab. HRC pushes interval stretching on *you*, and the rogue story is yours to tell or avoid.
3. **Chet after the collapse.**
   (a) *Hire him:* a visual inspector NPC who can cover a co-op station.
   (b) *Let him go:* postcards from other colonies, each describing a lab repeating the Annex, as a running dark joke.
4. **What is off-planet?**
   (a) *The Verdance Ring*, Lin's orbital seed garden.
   (b) *Phobos ice farms* that HRC wants to strip. You harvest carefully for the colony or fast for HRC, which brings Sallow back.
5. **Kestrel's secret.**
   (a) *A dismissed OIRM metrologist* whose standards are honest and whose name is broken.
   (b) *A fifth OIRM auditor* who went off-grid to investigate HRC and rejoins the team in the finale.
6. **Does the arc end?**
   (a) *Soft ending:* the arc closes and the farm runs on.
   (b) *Generational loop:* a second save where a new contractor trains at your old lab, now an OIRM site, with Trellis-H as the auditor.

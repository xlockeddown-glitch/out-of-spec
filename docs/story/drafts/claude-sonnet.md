# Within Tolerance

*A story arc for Out of Spec*

## 1. Title and logline

**Within Tolerance.** A contractor hired to put compliance stickers on a half-built Mars colony discovers that the only honest thing on the planet is a number you can trace, and slowly builds, one calibrated instrument at a time, the first farm on Mars that can prove it grew what it says it grew.

## 2. Premise

**Why you are here.** You are a calibration contractor, newly certified, newly in debt. Your last employer on Earth went under when an audit found that its "traceable" reference weights traced to a drawer. The only outfit still hiring calibration techs is **Brightwell-Nonesuch Habitats (BNH)** (new), the corporation behind Dome Station Perihelion-Adjacent, a colony that has been "ninety percent complete" for six years. BNH needs a certified contractor on site because its insurance, its launch licence and its investors all require *a sticker on everything*. It does not need the instruments to be right. It needs them stickered.

**The manager.** Your manager is **Marguerite Okonkwo-Tiff, "the Manager"** (new): a remote, relentlessly upbeat BNH middle-manager who exists mostly as a voice on the handheld phone, always on a different time zone's bad connection. She is not a villain. She is a person who has been told to hit a compliance number and has learned to confuse the number with the thing. She grants your one-time grace (the first audit that would fine you is a warning, and she recalibrates the offending instruments herself, badly but sincerely). Over the arc she is the character who changes most.

**BNH.** BNH sells "verified habitat living". Its slogan, painted on the dome, is *Every Sticker Counts*. Its real product is the sticker.

**OIRM.** The Orbital Institute of Reference Measures is a free-flying lab in high Earth orbit: the end of every traceability chain on the planet's side of the sky. It has no army and no budget for one. Its only power is that nobody can sell anything off-world without an OIRM-traceable certificate. OIRM wants one thing it can never say in a press release: it wants *one place on Mars where the chain is real*, so that it can prove traceability works at a distance of light-minutes. You are the candidate. You do not know it for most of the game.

**Inspector Vance** works for the colony authority and enforces OIRM traceability, arriving by drop pod every 5th morning.

## 3. Tone

Dry corporate comedy over a genuinely warm core. The jokes come from the gap between the sticker and the measurement; the stakes come from the same gap. A drifted probe means ugly turnips (funny); a drifted pressure gauge on the relay hab means a very quiet night and a very worried Orin (not funny, briefly). The rule of the arc: **every consequence is first absurd, then, if ignored, a little sad, never cruel.** Failure is a mess to tidy, not a punishment. The phone is the voice of the comedy: cheerful, passive-aggressive, and slightly too honest about its own errors. Competence is the reward and the joke is always on the paperwork, never on the player.

## 4. The arc in acts

### Act I: Stickers (Tier 0, PROVISIONAL, manual, small lab)

*The player does:* drop-pod arrival, the dome, the Pavonis Indexworks PX-1 rotary table and the Meridiani Dewpoint MD-2P humidity generator (Tier 0, certificates in hand), a first soil-moisture probe, the first rover, the first tarantula.

- **Beat 1: The welcome pack.** The Manager's welcome call lists your duties: calibrate everything, "especially the things that look fine." The dome smells of warm dust and solder. Your bench is a door on two crates.
- **Beat 2: Lin.** Dr. Lin, botanist, buys your first Biscuit Reeds and is terrified of the spider you keep in the dome. Her fear is traceable: a swarm of unlabelled lab spiders escaped in her postdoc years and she spent a night on a counter. She will not enter the conservatory. She buys crops at Bram's bay and leaves fast.
- **Beat 3: Bram.** The scrapyard mechanic fixes your rover with a hammer and a joyful disregard for torque. His rover works. Your torque wrench disagrees.
- **Beat 4: The first drift.** The irrigation trusts a drifting probe; the turnips come up off-spec and slightly purple. Lin buys them anyway, with commentary. The lesson arrives as comedy: the probe was wrong *by a bit*, and the bit cost you.
- **Set piece: The first audit.** Vance's pod lands. AUD-1T chimes. A sticker is crooked. Because it is your first, the Manager's grace triggers: a warning, and she "recalibrates" the violating instruments in a frantic phone montage. Two of them end up worse. You fix them before Vance reads the logs. He notices that your logs are *clean*. It is the first time anyone on the colony has handed him one.

### Act II: The Numbers Get Honest (PROVISIONAL to TRUSTED, human in the loop, medium lab)

*The player does:* bench work widens, the first contracts, the lab grows from small to medium, the first robot helper, a second rover, the first alien morph.

- **Beat 5: Trellis.** Bram builds a utility robot from scrap, rust-orange hull, one optical silk spool for the eye. TR-1 (new nickname **"Trell"**) is taught calibrations by watching you. It levels up by repetition and, crucially, it cannot calibrate *itself*. An out-of-tolerance robot taints every calibration it made. The robot teaches the player that automation grows the loop, not escapes it.
- **Beat 6: The first contract.** At the TRUSTED threshold OIRM offers a lower-uncertainty standard at a discount for a run of on-time calibrations. The phone shows the band change. Paid calibration jobs open up. The Manager sends a bouquet of stickers.
- **Beat 7: Chet.** The neon-vest rival contractor, who eyeballs everything and holds fake ISO certificates, secretly hires you to fix his jobs. He pays in credit and in a stream of increasingly specific excuses.
- **Beat 8: Old Orin.** The retired canyon geologist teaches underground relay beacons so thermal probes can report from the Basalt Thermal Tunnels. He asks for one thing: that the relay hab's pressure gauge be right, because he sleeps next to it.
- **Set piece: The Tunnel Day.** You drive a rover into the tunnels through a broken uplink. The arches break the signal; the lag spikes; the only thing keeping the sensors honest is a chain of beacons you calibrated yourself.
- **Lab upgrade: small to medium.** Bram knocks through a wall with an unnecessary amount of enthusiasm. The Manager sends a ribbon and a bill.

### Act III: The Chain (TRUSTED to ACCREDITED, remote operations, large lab)

*The player does:* a Starlink-like link goes live, the second and third alien morphs, the large lab, an intrinsic standard.

- **Beat 9: The link.** Remote control through an orbital relay ("the Sky Net", a fictional system (new)). Operations move from the handheld phone to a control panel; you can run the farm from the bench. The comedy changes: latency is now a character.
- **Beat 10: Kestrel.** The hooded orbital trader appears only on solar-flare nights, selling slings and gray-market reference standards with no OIRM certificate. Kestrel's secret (new): she is a former OIRM metrologist who left after discovering that the chain from Earth to Mars *had never been closed*; she sells uncertified standards because she thinks a standard you can inspect beats a certificate you can't. She is half right, which is the problem.
- **Beat 11: The audit team.** OIRM sends its robot master-auditors in person, as telepresence units: **CLADEUS**, **GEMINID**, **CHATTERLY-PT** and **SPACEFLUX-SI** (all new). They argue over every decimal. They are fair, funny and quietly terrifying. Each has a style: CLADEUS writes a ten-page gentle objection; GEMINID cross-checks against twelve sources and a vibe; CHATTERLY-PT is confidently wrong once and gracious about it; SPACEFLUX-SI launches things and apologises later.
- **Set piece: The Great Audit.** All four plus Vance plus AUD-1T in one dome, a day of recalls, one near-disaster (a drifting standard the player caught before anyone else), and a verdict: ACCREDITED.
- **Beat 12: The intrinsic standard.** A contract delivers an intrinsic standard, one that ends the traceability chain at your own bench instead of in orbit. You are now, in a small but real way, a place where the chain ends.

### Act IV: Off-Planet (ACCREDITED, remote rockets and robots)

*The player does:* AI rockets and robots, off-planet harvests, Bioluminescent Depths, the last alien morph.

- **Beat 13: The harvest.** With intrinsic standards and new skills you can send remote-controlled AI rockets and robots to harvest off-planet: crystal from a nearby moon, spore-silk from an orbital greenhouse. The comedy is that each rocket also needs a calibration certificate and a sticker.
- **Beat 14: Why.** The off-planet harvest is for *seed stock*: the Manager reveals that BNH wants to prove, at long last, that Mars can export something real. OIRM wants the same proof. For once the corporation and the institute want the same thing, and it is you.
- **Set piece: Blackout.** In the Bioluminescent Depths the orbital link drops entirely. Crystals shatter unless the drill is within tolerance. The player works by the calibration they did days earlier. The robot helper, now more human-like, finishes the job with them.

## 5. Cast arcs

- **Lin.** Starts as a spider-phobic buyer who leaves quickly. Her fear is a real incident, not a gag. Over the arc she moves from refusing the doorway, to standing in it, to holding a Crystal Weaver's silk up to the light and saying "that is *beautiful* tolerance". She fronts seed money when fines leave you broke, and eventually gets her own greenhouse. Her arc ends with her entering the conservatory on purpose.
- **Vance and AUD-1T.** Vance begins as a clipboard in a drop pod. Each clean log softens him. His reveal (new): he has been fining crooked stickers for six years because no one would give him a reason to do anything else. By Act III he is quietly on your side; by the end he signs a certificate with his full name. AUD-1T never changes its chime, and that is the point; the chime is a small constant. In the finale it chimes once, correctly, over a perfect log.
- **Bram.** Percussive maintenance to the end, but he learns torque, grudgingly: the first time a bolt he hit with a hammer actually fails, he buys a torque wrench and never mentions it. His gift is the robot hulls.
- **Kestrel.** The secret above. She ends as either an ally or a cautionary tale depending on a fork.
- **Chet.** Fake certificates, real heart. He is exposed in Act III when Vance's check fails his paper. He is humiliated, then hired as the player's field tech, and becomes proudly bad at honest work, then merely OK at it.
- **Old Orin.** The conscience of the arc. He teaches beacons and needs the relay hab's pressure gauge right. In the late game he walks the player through the tunnels one last time and admits he was a metrologist's apprentice once.
- **The robot helper (Trell).** Taught by watching, levelled by repetition. First it holds the tools. Later it does the simple calibrations. Then the complex ones. In Act IV a more human-like model arrives (the hull is the same; the manner is warmer). The emotional beat: it asks to be calibrated, because it understands that trusting itself is not the same as being right.
- **The OIRM auditors.** CLADEUS, GEMINID, CHATTERLY-PT, SPACEFLUX-SI. They begin as threats, then become colleagues. Each ends the arc having learned to say "I could be wrong by this much", which is the whole discipline.
- **New: Marguerite Okonkwo-Tiff, the Manager.** Her arc runs from sticker-obsessed to honestly proud. In the finale she asks for the real number instead of the sticker.
- **New: Pip (new),** a kid on the colony who asks why everything is blue and green. The player's answer, in tolerance bands, is the arc's emotional shorthand.

## 6. The antagonist, or central pressure

There is no single villain. The pressure is **the sticker**: the belief that a label can replace a measurement. It lives in BNH's compliance culture, in Chet's fake certificates, in Kestrel's gray market, and, most dangerously, in the player's own temptation to skip a calibration because the instrument "looks fine". Vance's fines are the symptom; drift is the cause.

The closest thing to a human face of it is BNH's regional director, **Director Halloran-Voss** (new), who wants the colony to *appear* complete for an investor visit. She does not want the player to fail; she wants them to be convenient. Her scheme is to push a "rapid certification" programme that stickers the whole colony without measuring anything. It collapses on a bad day, because physics doesn't read stickers.

## 7. The rogue lab

**How it works in story.** If you skip calibrations, instruments drift and the colony starts to go wonky. The narrative frames it as **"going rogue"**: the lab has left the chain. It scales with how far out of tolerance things are: small drift is a joke, large drift is a bigger joke with some misery.

**Concrete funny glitches (new):**
- Press left, the rover goes right. Press right and it politely declines.
- The irrigation waters the *rover* and leaves the crops thirsty.
- The phone reports the temperature as "a vibe" and the humidity as "yes".
- A drifted scale weighs Lin's Biscuit Reeds at "a lot", and she pays for "a lot".
- The tarantula's climate display insists the dome is both frozen and on fire; the spider, unbothered, is both.
- AUD-1T chimes on the wrong beat, then audits *itself*.
- A torque wrench "tightens" bolts by loosening them, to Bram's delighted horror.

**The misery (kept small).** Crops come out off-spec and sell for less; a fine arrives; the colony's lights stutter. Nothing is cruel, and every glitch leaves an obvious way back: calibrate it.

**How the story treats a rogue player.** With warmth and a raised eyebrow. Lin says, "Did you calibrate that?", then buys the crops anyway. Vance fines, then offers a form. The Manager says it is "a learning opportunity". The only real cost is time and credibility, and the road back is a calibration day. A player who stays rogue never gets a villain ending; they get the **Sticker Ending** (below), which is its own joke.

## 8. Specialization and lab upgrades

**Specialization.** The story supports a single-discipline route (for example only pressure and torque, or only electrical). It is harder, but it works: you still keep your own instruments honest, and you worry less about all the standards. The in-world logic is that colony customers pay for *the thing you are best at*. "You have to make money in many ways, or be really good at what you do," as Chet puts it, accidentally correctly.

- **The specialist's story (new):** you become the colony's one true authority on a single quantity. Contractors queue; the intrinsic standard arrives sooner for that discipline; the large lab is *one huge, quiet room* built around your best instrument.
- **The generalist's story:** you juggle everything. Your lab is many small rooms. You earn a little from everything and the audit team calls you "the colony's Swiss pocketknife", which is a compliment from CHATTERLY-PT and a threat from CLADEUS.

**Lab sizes.** *Small* is a door on crates (Act I). *Medium* is Bram knocking through a wall (Act II). *Large* is either one enormous hall for the specialist or a cluster of linked bays for the generalist (Act III). Each upgrade is celebrated with a ribbon, a bill and a very small party.

## 9. Co-op

**One player.** The story is a quiet duet: you, the phone, the Manager's voice, Lin at the bay. Every role is yours. The pacing is intimate and the jokes land as private asides.

**Four players.** The farm and dome are shared; one hosts. The story assigns *roles*: field (rovers and crops), bench (calibration), comms (the phone, the link, the Manager), and a floater. The Manager's calls address the farm as "team", and the shared wallet means Vance fines the farm and OIRM bills the farm. Personal skills and shared farm skills both advance. The day ends only when every player is in bed, so the story has a small recurring bit: the Manager's "good night, team" call waits, audibly, for the last player. Four players mean four different drifts to chase, four chances for the rogue lab to be funnier, and one very patient AUD-1T.

## 10. Endings

All endings are reached by the same standing: credibility, contracts and the closure of your chain.

- **The Closed Chain (best).** You hold an intrinsic standard; OIRM certifies your lab as a node where the chain truly ends. The Manager asks for the number instead of the sticker. Vance signs with his full name. A rocket you calibrated lifts off with the first real Mars export.
- **The Specialist's Peak.** You become the colony's single authority on one quantity. A quiet ending: a small plaque, a very large room, and a queue.
- **The Sticker Ending.** You went rogue and stayed there. The colony passes every audit on paper and nothing works. It is hilarious and a little sad: a dome full of perfect stickers and a very confused rover. The phone's last message is "Every Sticker Counts."

## 11. Ten sample lines

1. **Phone, orbital dispatch:** "Your soil-moisture probe is overdue by 3 d. The turnips have filed a concern."
2. **Phone, Manager:** "Great news! Your calibration sticker has been upgraded. The calibration itself has not."
3. **Vance:** "Your log is clean. I have never seen one. I will need a moment."
4. **AUD-1T (chime, then text):** "Tire pressure: 85 kPa [PLACEHOLDER]. Mood: compliant."
5. **Lin:** "The Biscuit Reeds are 12 % lighter than last week. Either you drifted or the reeds did."
6. **Bram:** "I hit it once. It was a 25 N·m bolt. Hit it twice. Now it's a 25 N·m *idea*."
7. **Orin:** "That gauge sits next to my pillow. Make it right, kid, and I'll sleep. Make it wrong and I won't."
8. **Kestrel:** "No certificate. Just a number you can poke. Take it or leave it, flare's ending in 40 min."
9. **CLADEUS:** "I have a mild objection. It is ten pages. It is very kind."
10. **Trell:** "Calibration complete: 5.0 mm (±0.1 mm) [PLACEHOLDER]. Please check me, I would like that."

## 12. Forks

1. **Kestrel's standard.** *(a)* Accept her uncertified reference and use it as your own chain's anchor, which is faster but fails the Great Audit unless you disclose it. *(b)* Refuse and wait for OIRM's certified standard, which is slower but clean.
2. **Chet's exposure.** *(a)* Report his fake certificates to Vance on day one, which ends his career. *(b)* Quietly fix his jobs and let him confess, which keeps him as an ally but delays the audit's clean slate.
3. **Director Halloran-Voss's rapid certification.** *(a)* Join the programme and sticker the colony, which pays well and feeds the Sticker Ending. *(b)* Refuse and calibrate properly, which costs the contract and builds credibility.
4. **Specialize or generalize.** *(a)* Go narrow: one discipline, one huge lab, an earlier intrinsic standard. *(b)* Stay broad: many small bays, steady income, a slower road to the chain's end.
5. **Trell's hull.** *(a)* Keep Trell the rust-orange utility robot, loyal and limited. *(b)* Upgrade to the more human-like model, who is better at the job and asks harder questions.
6. **The off-planet harvest.** *(a)* Spend the contract on seed stock, which gives Mars its first export. *(b)* Spend it on crystal, which funds the colony's own relay array and keeps the harvest local.

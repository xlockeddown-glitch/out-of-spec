# The Silk Standard

The story of *Out of Spec*, merged from four drafts (`drafts/`) along the path Ryan chose on 2026-11-22 (`docs/decisions.md`, "Story path"). Where a beat comes from one draft, the draft is named. Items marked **(proposed)** are the agent's fill-ins for Ryan to confirm or change; they are not his calls. Real specs stay [PLACEHOLDER]. Player-facing lines follow NIST SP 811.

**Logline.** Indentured by fine print to a half-built Mars colony, a metrology contractor keeps a corporation's instruments honest, breeds the tarantulas whose silk holds them in tolerance, and ends by shipping the first thing Mars ever exported that OIRM will vouch for: the silk itself.

## 1. Premise

**Why you are here.** Brightwell-Nonesuch Habitats (BNH), slogan *Every Sticker Counts*, ran a "Zero-Down Homesteading" drive on Earth. Page 47(b) of the paperwork turned your calibration certificate into a five-year colony metrology contract. You arrive at **Pavonis Shelf Station** (proposed) with two Tier 0 standards and their OIRM certificates, the **Pavonis Indexworks PX-1** rotary table and the **Meridiani Dewpoint MD-2P** humidity generator, plus one Curlyhair in a takeout box. The return berth in your contract is listed as "available, billable". The tone is fine-print comedy, not hardship: the joke is BNH's paperwork, never the player's misery.

**Why BNH needs you.** BNH's charter lets it sell nothing off Mars unless the measurements behind it trace to OIRM. It is the one rule BNH cannot lobby away. The colony's last contractor (Kestrel, §4) left two years ago and the stickers expired quietly. BNH does not need the instruments to be right. It needs them stickered.

**The manager.** **Odalys Brandt** (proposed name), orbital dispatch, signs every notification "Warmly, Dispatch". She covers six colonies alone. She was a bench metrologist before she was promoted out of touching instruments (Fable). The one-time grace is hers: the night before the first audit that would fine you, she rides the courier pod down against policy, recalibrates the violating instruments, and is gone before Vance lands. You find the bench warm, your tools in a better order and a mug ring on the logbook. She never mentions it.

**OIRM.** The Orbital Institute of Reference Measures keeps the primary standards. It has no army and no interest in BNH's stickers. What it wants is a second root: one surface lab trustworthy enough to hold an intrinsic standard, so traceability chains no longer all end at one lab in Earth orbit. Your credibility rating is, without your knowing it, an application.

**The pressure.** There is no villain to defeat. The antagonist is drift, and the standing temptation to buy the sticker without the measurement. BNH is that temptation with a budget; Chet is that temptation with a face you like; the rogue path is that temptation in your own hands at 23:00 with four instruments due.

## 2. The tarantula spine

Ryan's rule: tarantulas run through the whole arc, not just the dome. Three threads carry it.

1. **Each act's key unlock needs a new morph's silk.** The canon silk effects (`docs/canon.md`) decide which morph fits where, in order of tightening climate band (proposed):

   | Act | Morph | Silk (canon) | What it unlocks in the story |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | I | Curlyhair, then Cobalt Sentinel | Optical silk | Jig-1's eye; the first rover lag cut |
   | II | Terracotta Knee | Anti-static dust filaments | A rover fleet that holds steering in dust; Orin's tunnel beacons |
   | III | Crystal Weaver, Orbital Silk-Spinner | Resonant emerald fibres; zero-expansion reticle silk | The Depths drill; the Gossamer link antenna and multi-rover autopilot |
   | IV | Basalt Thermal-Vent | Mag-shielded thermal resin | Rockets and robots that work in the magnetic dead zone and in orbit |

2. **The intrinsic standard is silk.** It is built from Crystal Weaver resonant fibre held in Silk-Spinner reticle silk (proposed; the quantity it realizes is [PLACEHOLDER]). The traceability chain literally ends in your spiders.
3. **Spiders go off-planet.** The off-planet site cannot be kept in tolerance from Mars by robots alone. It needs a spider colony spinning on site, so you breed and ship one.

## 3. The arc

Four acts on the three credibility bands. ACCREDITED is the top band. Each act opens on a gameplay threshold, not a date.

### Act I: "Every Sticker Counts"

*PROVISIONAL · small lab · manual · Tier 0 · crops only · Terracotta Plateau*

The lab is a supply closet off the conservatory. Everything is by hand: carry the soil probe in, check it against the MD-2P, write the sticker, steer the one shared rover over a link that answers late.

- **The thirsty probe.** Auto-irrigation trusts a drifted soil-moisture probe. The Dust Turnips look fine on the phone and wrong in the crate. Lin, buying at Bram's bay, weighs one in her hand: "Your probe is lying to you." This is the thesis of the game, delivered by a root vegetable (Fable).
- **The grace audit (set piece).** Day 5, 06:00. Vance's drop pod lands and AUD-1T chimes at a crooked sticker. The fine you expected is a warning, because of the warm bench and the mug ring.
- **Lin won't come in.** She buys at Bram's bay and will not enter the dome. Spiders.
- **Chet.** Neon vest, a van-rover with CERTIFIED on the side in three fonts. He undercuts you, then a week later slips you an envelope to fix one of his jobs.
- **First flare night.** The link drops and the sky goes green. Kestrel is at the dome door with a case of reference standards and no paperwork.
- **Jig-1 opens its eye.** Bram welds a wobbly scrap cart. Its eye costs one optical silk spool from your Curlyhair (Gemini). The first alien morph, Cobalt Sentinel, arrives from Kestrel and doubles the spool rate.
- **Lin fronts seed money** the first time fines empty the wallet.

**Act end:** a run of on-time calibrations reaches TRUSTED, and the phone shows the new band.

### Act II: "Trust, but Verify the Robot"

*TRUSTED · medium lab · human in the loop · first contract · paid jobs · Basalt Thermal Tunnels*

- **The first contract.** OIRM offers a lower-uncertainty pressure standard, the HB-700 [PLACEHOLDER], at a discount for a run of on-time calibrations. Vance carries it in from his pod himself.
- **Greenhouse Four.** BNH won't fund an expansion, so Lin and Bram co-sign for the only empty pressurized shell: the sealed greenhouse where Lin's incident happened (§4). Lin unlocks it with her eyes mostly shut. It becomes your medium lab (Fable).
- **Jig-2.** You teach it by calibrating while it watches. It proposes each reading and waits for your tap: accept or reject. That tap is the human in the loop. Override its safety interlock to clear a backlog and Vance fines the farm (Gemini).
- **Terracotta Knee.** Its anti-static filaments let a second and third rover hold steering in the dust, and they wrap the relay beacons Old Orin teaches you to plant under the basalt arches.
- **Certainty Plus.** BNH buys Chet's outfit and rebrands it **Certainty Plus** (proposed name). Chet gets a title and a bigger vest. The lab runs "predictive compliance": instruments are presumed in tolerance and stickered in bulk. Chet stops slipping you envelopes because he is no longer allowed to admit his jobs need fixing.
- **AUD-1T's gauge.** A paid job reveals that the tire gauge AUD-1T has fined people with for years was never calibrated. It asks, in its chime voice, to be calibrated. Its fines become correct, which is worse (Fable).
- **Specialization.** Paid jobs sort by discipline. See §6.

**Act end:** Certainty Plus goes live colony-wide.

### Act III: "Project Sunsetting"

*TRUSTED to ACCREDITED · large lab · remote · OIRM master audit · Bioluminescent Depths*

- **The deadline (set piece).** A BNH priority memo: the colony is flagged under **Project Sunsetting** (proposed name). It will be replaced by uncalibrated strip-mining drones unless your lab passes a full OIRM master audit within [PLACEHOLDER] d, proving Mars can run an export node (Gemini).
- **Certainty Plus goes wonky.** Every instrument it touched is overdue at once. Airlocks open politely to the wrong side, the canteen scale reports "approx.", and rovers that aren't yours turn left when told right. Your farm, if you kept it clean, is the one green island, and people start arriving at your door.
- **Crystal Weaver and the Depths.** Total orbital blackout. Crystals shatter unless the drill is within sub-percent tolerance. Only Crystal Weaver fibre holds the drill steady. You work on beacons, by hand and nerve.
- **The Gossamer link.** A relay constellation gives the colony a link that doesn't lag, but its ground antenna needs Orbital Silk-Spinner reticle silk. The last piece of the colony's infrastructure waits on your spiders. From the control panel you run rovers and benches you aren't standing at.
- **Kestrel's case.** Vance spots her uncertified standard on your bench. The truth comes out (§4).
- **The master audit (set piece).** An OIRM shuttle lands CLADEUS, GEMINID, CHATTERLY-PT and SPACEFLUX-SI. For two days they cross-examine your chain, the conservatory climate logs against the drill logs. BNH orders Vance to accept Certainty Plus stickers. He holds one up, peels it off and files it as evidence.
- **Jig-3 fails its own check.** Mid-audit the robot is found out of tolerance. It quarantines its own recent work and lists every calibration it touched as suspect. The auditors mark it as the most creditable thing they see all week (Fable).
- **Odalys goes off script.** Her notifications lose the cheer and gain content. She sends you the dispatch instrument list she isn't supposed to share.

**Act end:** ACCREDITED. Project Sunsetting is "paused for learnings". Certainty Plus folds. Chet takes whatever errands you'll give him.

### Act IV: "The Silk Standard"

*ACCREDITED · large lab · fully remote · intrinsic standard · off-planet harvest*

- **The intrinsic contract.** OIRM offers an intrinsic standard as a primary. It is silk: Crystal Weaver fibre in Silk-Spinner reticle (proposed). The chain on your phone, which has always scrolled off the screen toward orbit, now stops at your conservatory.
- **Basalt Thermal-Vent.** The last morph has the tightest band in the game. Its mag-shielded resin is what lets rockets and robots work in the magnetic dead zone and in orbit.
- **Sora-7.** Bram builds the humanoid model. It asks for its own OIRM credential. You choose (§5).
- **The Larder.** A robot greenhouse on Phobos (proposed). Robots you trained run it, but robots alone can't keep a site in tolerance from this far away. It needs spiders spinning on site, so you breed a Silk-Spinner colony and ship it up with the substrate.
- **First export (set piece).** BNH wants crops. OIRM wants proof. The first cargo that passes OIRM is certified tarantula silk, measured against the silk standard. The crops ride the same rocket. Lin seals the crates. SPACEFLUX-SI calls the countdown.

## 4. Cast

**Dr. Lin.** Years ago she was the junior botanist locked overnight in Greenhouse Four when its hygrometer read fine while the room dried out. The conservatory's Curlyhairs came through the vents looking for moisture and found her lettuce. She has blamed spiders ever since; her arc is learning it was the hygrometer (Fable). She buys your crops at Bram's bay, unlocks Greenhouse Four in Act II, holds silk up to the light in Act III, and in Act IV seals the silk crates. She sells the mosses that unlock the higher morphs.

**Kestrel.** She was the contractor before you. She refused to sign off Greenhouse Four's sensors without a recalibration, was overruled by BNH, and took the blame when they failed. Her certification was pulled. Her gray-market standards are honest; she just can't sign them (Fable). She comes on flare nights because the monitoring is down, and sells you slings and morphs. Her arc ends as the off-world dealer for *your* tarantulas: once your silk standard verifies her references, she trades your slings across the system under her own name again.

**Inspector Vance and AUD-1T.** Vance fines crooked stickers because stickers are all BNH lets him check. He was a real inspector once. Clean traceability logs are the first thing in years worth reading, and he starts arriving early to read them. When BNH orders him to accept Certainty Plus stickers, he refuses in the driest way possible: by following OIRM's rules exactly (Fable). AUD-1T asks to be calibrated after the gauge incident and wears its own sticker dead straight.

**Bram.** Percussive maintenance, no respect for torque specs, until Jig-2's arm, which he tightened by feel, falls off in front of Vance. He brings you his torque wrench in a rag like a sick pet. He builds every Jig hull, Sora-7 and the rocket cradle.

**Chet.** Rival, then secret client, then Certainty Plus's corporate mascot. After it folds he runs errands for the farm, humbler, still guessing measurements by eye and still wrong (Gemini).

**Old Orin.** He signed the colony's original site survey with an instrument he knew was overdue, and the colony sits some distance from where it should (distance [PLACEHOLDER]); that is why the water and the good crystal are deep and far. The beacons are his penance. He confesses in the tunnels, to you and to the logbook (Fable).

**Odalys Brandt.** §1. Her arc ends with a transfer request she has had in drafts for two years, and she walks into the lab she once fixed in the dark.

## 5. The robot helper: Jig to Sora-7

Jig-1 is a wobbly scrap cart with one optical silk eye that fetches stickers and announces each one in monotone. Jig-2 proposes readings for your approval. Jig-3 runs routine calibrations across benches. **Sora-7** is humanoid, with dry opinions about Chet's eyesight, and it refuses to let Bram near its wrist encoders without a signed torque log (Gemini).

None of them ever calibrates itself; that is always you. Late in Act IV, Sora-7 asks for its own OIRM credential. **The player chooses:** sponsor its exam so it works without your sign-off, or keep the human-in-the-loop lock. Either way it is still calibrated by someone else.

## 6. Specialization and the lab

Ryan's call stands as gameplay: a single-discipline route is harder but works, and you still keep your own site's instruments in tolerance. In Act II paid jobs sort by discipline (pressure and torque, climate, electrical, dimensional). A generalist earns many ways; a specialist is really good at one thing. A specialist sends out-of-scope work to OIRM in Vance's pod, or to Chet with you checking his results.

The lab tells the same story: the **closet** (small), **Greenhouse Four** (medium: a few big bays or several small rooms), and the large lab (one great hall for a specialist, a campus of rooms for a generalist), with the conservatory always at its heart.

## 7. The rogue lab

Going rogue isn't a button. It is what a lab becomes when instruments are used overdue, and it gets wonkier the further out they drift and the more depends on them. It is funny first, with some misery.

**Glitches:**
- The rover turns right when you press left.
- Dust Turnips grow square.
- It rains indoors, in a column, over the bench only.
- Jig stickers rocks and Bram's toaster IN SPEC.
- The phone's own clock drifts: "It is probably morning."
- Stickers slowly rotate on their own, which AUD-1T finds deeply upsetting.

**The spiders notice first.** Out of their bands they sulk and stop spinning, so the silk-dependent unlocks degrade: the rover lag comes back, the Gossamer link stutters. Then they start building where they shouldn't: webs over sensors, a Curlyhair hoarding stickers in its hide.

**The misery.** Fines grow. Off-spec crops sell as novelties. Lin stops fronting money and says why, once. ACCREDITED, the intrinsic contract and the Larder stay locked.

**The way back** is always open: **the Long Recal**, a multi-day chain where you recalibrate everything, rebuild the trace and pass a hard audit with no grace. Labs that return earn a **"Returned to Spec"** stamp on their certificates (Opus).

## 8. Co-op

BNH's contract is for "one (1) certified contractor", so 2 to 4 players are one contractor, plural: one wallet, one rating, one host farm. Vance fines the farm and OIRM bills the farm. Dispatch can't close the day until every signatory is in bed, which Odalys calls "horizontally compliant" (Fable).

- **Roles:** field, bench, comms and conservatory. Personal skills plus farm skills let four specialists make one generalist farm.
- **Rogue is a farm state.** One player skipping calibrations wonks the rover for everyone.
- **The master audit:** one auditor shadows each player; smaller crews get doubled-up auditors.
- **Solo:** Jig and the townsfolk fill in.

## 9. Endings

The farm runs on after any ending; these are where the arc lands.

1. **The Silk Standard (main).** The Larder's first harvest comes down, and the first OIRM-passed Mars export goes up: certified silk, with the crops beside it. Project Sunsetting is cancelled. Kestrel takes the first crate of slings off-world. Odalys walks into the lab.
2. **The Ultimate Breed.** For players who master both spiders and metrology: a late breeding chain crosses all five alien morphs into one line with every silk trait (name proposed: the **Standard-Bearer**). Its climate band is the tightest in the game, so only a fully calibrated dome can hold it.
3. **The Breeder.** You let metrology slide to TRUSTED and become Mars's tarantula breeder. The colony gets by; Kestrel sells your slings across the system.
4. **The Spiders Run the Dome (rogue).** Out-of-band climate lets the spiders take over: webs over every sensor, stickers hoarded in their hides, and BNH certifies the dome as a "wildlife compliance zone". The last note is from Lin. Then the game offers the Long Recal, always.
